# Writing Guide: Essays, Resume, Emails

## Essay coaching (default: coach mode)

The student writes; Claude coaches. Before starting, check the program's AI policy and report it.

**1. Unpack the prompt.** Restate what the readers are really asking. Program essays usually test
one of: *genuine curiosity*, *ability to do independent work*, *how you handle not knowing*,
*what you'd add to the cohort*.

**2. Brainstorm from the profile.** Offer 3-4 angles, each tied to a specific real thing in
`profile.md` (a project, a failure, a question that bugged them). Ask follow-up questions to dig
out details the profile doesn't have: *What exactly went wrong? What did you try next? What do you
still not understand?* The best material usually comes from these answers.

**3. Outline.** For the chosen angle: hook (a concrete moment), what they did, what they learned
or still wonder about, why this program is the next step (specific: a course, a lab, a format).

**4. Feedback on the student's draft.** Save the draft to
`applications/<slug>/essays/<n>-draft-v<k>.md`, then give:
- 2-3 biggest-impact changes first (structure, specificity, answering the actual prompt)
- Places where a claim needs a concrete example
- Generic phrases to cut ("I have always been passionate about...", "ever since I was young")
- Word count vs. limit
- Line edits for grammar/clarity as *suggestions*, keeping the student's word choices where they
  work. Never replace their voice with a polished adult voice - admissions readers notice, and
  it's the student's application.

**Program-specific tips**
- Research programs: show you can work through a hard problem, not that you already know the
  answer. Name a specific question you'd like to investigate.
- Humanities seminars (TASS etc.): intellectual honesty and real engagement with an idea beat
  impressive vocabulary.
- Math programs: the application problems matter most. Coach with hints and questions, never
  solutions - submitting someone else's solutions is cheating and programs check.

## Activities resume (one page)

Can be drafted directly, from profile facts only. Save as `applications/<slug>/resume.md` and
`resume.html` (print-friendly, see below).

Sections in order: Name + contact (email only by default; ask before adding phone/address),
Education (school, grad year, GPA if strong, relevant courses), Research & Projects, Activities &
Leadership, Work & Volunteering, Awards, Skills.

Bullets: verb + what + measurable result. "Built a Python script that sorted 2,000 bird
photos by species with 87% accuracy" beats "Worked on a machine learning project". Tailor order
and emphasis to the program; never add anything not in the profile.

For `resume.html`: a single self-contained file, system fonts, 0.6in margins, `@page { size: letter; margin: 0.5in }`,
no external assets. Tell the student to open it and use *Print → Save as PDF*, and check it fits
on one page.

## Emails (recommendation requests, cold outreach, follow-ups)

Drafted directly; the student sends them. Short (under 150 words), specific, polite, easy to say
yes to.

**Recommendation request** - ask in person if possible, then follow with an email including:
program name and link, deadline, how the letter is submitted, 2-3 things the student hopes the
teacher can speak to, and the student's resume. Ask at least **3-4 weeks** before the deadline.

**Thank-you notes** after interviews and after recommenders submit.
