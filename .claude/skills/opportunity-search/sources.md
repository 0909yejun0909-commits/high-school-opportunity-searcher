# Where High School Opportunities Live

Search these in addition to `data/programs_seed.json`. Prefer the organization's own page over
any list or blog; lists are for discovery only. Sections 1-3 are US-focused examples of *kinds*
of sources - every country has equivalents (universities, national labs, hospitals, museums,
city youth programs). Section 4 covers students outside the US and anyone going abroad.

## 1. Directories (discovery, US)
- **Pathways to Science** - pathwaystoscience.org - searchable STEM programs, filter by
  "High School". Good for research and summer programs.
- **University pre-college / outreach pages** - search `"<university> high school summer research"`,
  `"<university> pre-college program"` for universities in or near the student's state.
- **Government** - intern.nasa.gov, navalsteminterns.us, usaeop.com, afrlscholars.usra.edu,
  training.nih.gov (high school), nist.gov/ship, doe national labs ("<lab> high school internship"
  e.g. Argonne, Oak Ridge, Fermilab, Brookhaven, Los Alamos, Sandia, NREL, LLNL, PNNL),
  usajobs.gov (filter: students).
- **State** - "<state> governor's school", state science academy, state university system
  summer programs, state department of education enrichment lists.

## 2. Local (often the best odds)
Search with the student's **city/region**, never their name:
- Hospitals and medical centers: "teen volunteer", "high school research internship"
- Museums, zoos, aquariums, science centers, botanical gardens: "teen program", "youth intern"
- City youth employment programs (e.g. summer youth employment program in large cities) - paid
- Public library teen programs, community colleges (dual enrollment, summer courses)
- Local companies/startups: "<city> high school internship" - apply the legitimacy check hard
- Local university labs - these are usually reached by cold email (`/outreach`), not postings

## 3. Field-specific
- **Math:** Ross, PROMYS, Mathcamp, HCSSiM, SUMaC, MIT PRIMES, AwesomeMath, MathILy,
  BU/Stanford/Yale-affiliated math circles. Search "math summer program high school application problems".
- **CS / AI:** university CS outreach, company high school programs (verify the current year -
  these come and go), USACO camp, Girls Who Code, AI4ALL.
- **Biomed / life science:** NIH, JAX, Broad, Rockefeller, Fred Hutch, cancer centers, state
  medical schools' "high school summer research".
- **Engineering:** BWSI, DoD lab apprenticeships, university engineering summer camps.
- **Environment / field science:** fisheries/wildlife agencies, Hutton, Student Conservation
  Association (high school crews), national parks youth programs.
- **Humanities / writing / journalism:** TASS, Iowa Young Writers, Kenyon, Princeton Summer
  Journalism, local newspaper/radio youth programs.
- **Policy / business / leadership:** YYGS, Bank of America Student Leaders, Boys/Girls State,
  Senate/House page programs, local government youth councils.

## 4. International & abroad

**Outside the US: search like a local.** Use the country's terms (UK: "Year 12", "sixth form",
"work experience", "widening participation summer school"; Australia: "Year 11", "school
holidays"; Singapore: "JC", "upper secondary"; France: "lycée", "stage") and the local language
(German "Schülerakademie", "Praktikum für Schüler"; Spanish "programa de verano para
estudiantes de secundaria"; Korean, Japanese, Portuguese...). Most national programs are only
described in the local language.

**Where to look in any country**
- Universities' outreach / "widening participation" / pre-university pages, and national
  research institutes' education pages (search "<institute> high school students" or the local
  equivalent)
- National science/maths olympiad and science-fair organizers - many run summer camps for
  finalists
- Ministry of education or national science-agency youth programs
- Embassy and cultural-institute exchange scholarships (e.g. Goethe-Institut, British Council,
  Fulbright commissions) - verify each on its own site

**Programs to look up by country/region** (names to verify, not facts):
- **UK:** UNIQ (Oxford), Sutton Trust Summer Schools, In2scienceUK, Smallpeice Trust,
  Headstart, university widening-participation programs, PROMYS Europe
- **Canada:** Shad, university enrichment camps, Youth Science Canada (science fairs)
- **Europe:** PROMYS Europe (Oxford, open to students resident in Europe and Mediterranean
  countries), CERN Beamline for Schools (worldwide teams), national olympiad camps,
  Germany: Deutsche SchülerAkademie, Jugend forscht
- **Israel:** Weizmann Institute ISSI (graduating seniors worldwide)
- **Australia:** National Youth Science Forum (runs in January)
- **Singapore:** A*STAR Science Awards (school nomination)
- **India:** PROMYS India; national olympiad camps

**US programs that accept international students** (check each one's international page:
separate deadlines, English tests, aid, visitor visa): RSI (via a national selection agency),
SSP, Ross, PROMYS, Canada/USA Mathcamp, SUMaC, YYGS, TASS (sophomores only from abroad).
Most US government and lab programs (NASA, NIH, DoD, NIST) are US citizens only.

**Exchanges** (a semester or year abroad, often fully funded):
- From the US: NSLI-Y, Congress-Bundestag Youth Exchange (Germany), Kennedy-Lugar YES Abroad
- *To* the US from eligible countries: Kennedy-Lugar YES and FLEX (US State Department) - check
  which countries are eligible this year
- Worldwide: AFS Intercultural Programs and Rotary Youth Exchange (local clubs/chapters select)

**Aggregator sites** (Opportunity Desk, HeySuccess, "top 15 programs" blogs) are useful for
discovery but are often out of date and sometimes advertise paid programs as scholarships -
always go to the official page, and apply the legitimacy check.

## 5. Online / school-year research
For students who can't travel: PRIMES-USA, BWSI online prerequisites, MITES Semester, remote
mentoring through cold emails, and citizen-science projects (Zooniverse) that can grow into
independent projects.

## Search hygiene
- Add the **target break's year** to queries (e.g. "2027") and reject pages showing only old cycles
  unless the program clearly runs every year - then mark the dates unverified.
- Skip: undergraduate-only REUs (most require college enrollment - check, a few accept graduating
  seniors), pages behind logins, and lead-generation listicles that just funnel to a paid service.
